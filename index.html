Chocolate Sales Analytics

Capstone, 3MTT / DeepTech_Ready Data Science & AI programme. Python, SQL, Power BI.

Problem: A chocolate company sells in 6 countries through 25 sales reps and 22 products, but had no single place to see top markets, products or performers.

3,282transactions cleaned
$19.79Mtotal sales analysed
12DAX measures
7EDA charts
  • Python: converted Amount from text ("$5,320.00") to numbers, converted dates to datetimes, checked for missing and duplicate values, built 7 exploratory charts.
  • SQL: queries for core metrics, top 5 products, sales by country and rep, and year-over-year growth (+7.44% in 2023, +4.83% in 2024).
  • Power BI: star-schema model (calendar table, sales fact table, measures table) and a one-page dashboard with KPIs, filters and drill-down.

Findings

Revenue is spread evenly across all six countries. The overall top rep leads in only one market, so the business has six regional leaders, not one star. Canada earns $32.35 per box against $36 to $40 elsewhere, which is worth investigating.

Recommendations

Look into Canada's pricing and product mix, reward regional top performers, and use 5 to 7% as the realistic growth baseline for targets.

Deliverables: Python notebook, SQL script, Power BI dashboard (.pbix), full written report.

Nigeria Out-of-School Children Dashboard, 2021–2024

Power BI dashboard, 3 pages. Data cleaned in Excel, modelled in Power BI.

Problem: Millions of Nigerian children are out of school, and the picture is hard to see by state, zone and year. I built a dashboard to show where the gaps are and what sits behind them.

64Mout-of-school children
33%weighted out-of-school rate
192Mschool-age population
58.54%average MPI poverty
  • Data model: a state table, a year table, a fact table of out-of-school records (148 rows, 25 fields) and a separate measures table.
  • Fields covered: primary and JSS out-of-school rates, enrollment by gender, gender parity index, pupil-teacher ratio, pupil-classroom ratio and poverty headcount.
  • What it shows: the North-West and North-East carry the largest out-of-school totals, and the state table lets a reader compare class sizes and poverty side by side.

The dataset's source column reads "NBS / UNICEF MICS6 / UBEC Synthetic Calibration", so figures are modelled, not raw survey results.

Police Recorded Crime: Cleaning and Merging 12 Years of Data

Data cleaning, merging and validation in Excel and Python (pandas), 2012/13 to 2023/24.

Problem: Twelve yearly sheets of UK police recorded crime data had to become one reliable dataset. The first merge looked finished but hid errors that changed the results.

12 → 1yearly sheets merged
258,905rows checked against sheet totals
59.5Moffences analysed
0nulls or duplicates left

What I found and fixed

  • The 2016/17 sheet had every column shifted one place right. That left 21,308 rows with no offence counts and put offence descriptions in the Force Name column. I realigned the fields and recovered 4.97M offences the first merge had counted as zero.
  • Replaced "Unknown" text in a numeric column with real whole numbers, standardised text and year formats, and confirmed there were no duplicates.
  • The first summary ranked offence groups by row count, not by offences. Corrected, Theft leads with 20.1M (34%), then Violence against the person with 15.5M (26%) and Fraud with 8.3M (14%).
  • Separated 43 territorial police forces from British Transport Police and four fraud reporting bodies, so they are not compared like-for-like.

Recorded offences by financial year (millions)

2023/24 contains only the first quarter, so it is not comparable with full years.

Findings

Recorded offences rose from 4.06M in 2012/13 to a high of 6.74M in 2022/23. The one clear drop is 2020/21, about 11% below the year before.

Deliverables: cleaned dataset, a summary report built with formulas, and a cleaning log listing every issue and fix.
